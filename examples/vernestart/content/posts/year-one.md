Twelve months ago I started this site to give my technical writing a
home that wasn't a notes app or a closed platform. Here is what
happened.

## What stuck

- **A weekly cadence**, give or take. Some weeks were lighter, but the
  rhythm held. The first version of every post lived in a notebook for
  at least a day before it got published.
- **A short list of topics.** Writing about a few things deeply beats
  writing about everything shallowly. I learned more by going back to
  the same problem from a different angle than I ever did chasing the
  next.
- **Replies by email.** A handful of conversations that started in my
  inbox were genuinely the best part of writing in public.

## What didn't

- **Cross-posting** to social platforms. I tried for two months. The
  return was a few extra clicks and a feeling of obligation; not worth
  it.
- **Comments embedded in the page.** I ran a static comment system for
  a week. Spam, moderation, latency. Email turned out to be a feature.
- **Long lead times.** The drafts that sat for more than a couple of
  weeks rarely shipped. Better to publish something rough than to wait
  for a polish that never comes.

## What I'd do again

Write the way I want to read. Resist the urge to optimise for an
audience I can't see. Keep the toolchain boring and the page light.
Hit publish.

Twelve more months, then.
