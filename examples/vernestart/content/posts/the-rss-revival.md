I subscribe to seventy-three feeds. Not seventy-three followed accounts,
seventy-three RSS feeds. Some are blogs. Some are podcasts. One is a
weather station in Reykjavík that posts a snowfall reading every six
hours. They all arrive in the same reader, in the same order, with no
algorithm deciding what I get to see.

## The promise that survived

RSS was supposed to be irrelevant by 2010. It was, briefly. Then social
networks went hostile to outbound traffic, walled gardens started
charging for engagement, and the indie web turned out to be where most of
my favourite reading lives. Feeds stopped looking obsolete and started
looking *robust*.

A thing that has been "dying" since the year I was born is, at this
point, more durable than most of what tried to bury it.

## What works

- **No login.** The reader pulls; the publisher publishes. No account
  recovery flow. No 2FA. No password reset email gone to spam.
- **Full content if you want it.** Plenty of feeds ship the whole post.
  No tracking pixel, no click-through.
- **Order is chronological.** Newest first. Always. No mystery box.

## What I miss

Honest answer? Comments. The reading experience is great; the
*conversation* is fragmented. Some feeds link out to a comment thread
elsewhere. That works. Most don't, and you're back in your inbox if you
want to say something.

It's a fair trade. I'd take a thoughtful email reply over forty drive-by
takes any day.
